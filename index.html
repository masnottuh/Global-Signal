ABOUT

Official sources, a method you can check

The first product is a US Economic Health Index and a monthly note that walks through it. Only official government sources go into this report.

What goes in

Six official series go into the index: real GDP, the unemployment rate, nonfarm payrolls, the consumer price index, the effective federal funds rate, and industrial production. Each series is converted into a simple change, compared with its own recent history, and averaged with equal weights. The result is a 0–100 reading and a regime label: expansion, slowdown, or contraction.

How we publish it

We publish the formula, the observation month, and the data vintage, including when a figure is still a preliminary or advance estimate. This is research based on public data, not a prediction of market prices and not a substitute for reading the original government releases.

Official sources

Inputs come from the Bureau of Economic Analysis, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, and the Federal Reserve Board.

Equal weights

Each of the six series is compared with its own recent history and averaged with equal weights. No hidden model.

Vintages recorded

We publish the observation month and the data vintage, including preliminary or advance estimates.

Not a forecast

The index is a contemporaneous reading of official statistics, not a prediction of market prices.

Our commitment: Every figure is cited back to an official release. We do not use satellite imagery, social media, news-feed sentiment, or unofficial price scrapes in this report.

THE INDEX

The US Economic Health Index

The index is updated after the official monthly and quarterly releases, not in real time. A reading of 50 means the latest changes are in line with each series’ trailing ten-year history. It is not a claim that conditions are “normal,” and it is not a forecast.

Historical official data. A sample report, “GlobalSignal US Economic Health, November 2026,” is in preparation for waitlist readers. It includes the current reading, the official inputs, a chart, and the method.

BEA

Real GDP

Quarterly real gross domestic product, used as a change versus its own recent history. Advance and later estimates are labeled by vintage.

BLS

Unemployment rate

The official civilian unemployment rate, compared with its trailing history rather than treated as a raw level.

BLS

Nonfarm payrolls

Total nonfarm payroll employment, including when the latest print is still a preliminary estimate.

BLS

Consumer price index

CPI-U, converted into a simple change and signed so that faster inflation weighs as a drag on the reading.

Federal Reserve

Effective federal funds rate

The effective federal funds rate, included as a change versus its own recent history.

Federal Reserve

Industrial production

The Federal Reserve Board’s industrial production index, equally weighted with the other five series.
