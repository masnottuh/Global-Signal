Free official-data signals on the US economy

Global Signal is a free hub of official US government statistics. We publish a transparent US Economic Health Index, daily and weekly readings from the Treasury yield curve, jobless claims, and the Federal Reserve’s broad dollar index, and we are preparing a free TradingView indicator. Every figure is cited to the Bureau of Economic Analysis, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the Department of the Treasury, the Department of Labor, or the Federal Reserve Board. This is not a trading-alert service, and we do not use satellite imagery, social media, or unofficial price scrapes.

ABOUT

Official sources, a method you can check

Everything on this site is free. The aim is a clear, sourced reading of official data, not a paid newsletter and not a buy or sell recommendation.

What you will find here

A small set of official-data signals, each tied to the agency that produces the underlying series. The flagship monthly reading is the US Economic Health Index. Alongside it we report the Treasury yield curve, weekly jobless claims, and the broad US dollar index as official observations arrive. A free TradingView indicator is in draft and is not published yet. Read the full methodology for the index, including sources, vintages, data gaps, and the limits of the historical NBER comparison.

How the figures are handled

We publish the observation date, the official source, and the vintage, including when a figure is still a preliminary or advance estimate. Missing official observations are left as gaps; they are not interpolated or filled. Derived comparisons, such as a yield spread from two official par yields, are labeled as such. This is research based on public data, not a prediction of market prices and not a substitute for reading the original government releases.

Official sources

Inputs come from the Bureau of Economic Analysis, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the Department of the Treasury, the Department of Labor, and the Federal Reserve Board.

A method you can check

The index formula, regime bands, vintages, and known gaps are on the methodology page. Nothing here is a black-box score.

Free to use

The signals, the index, and the planned TradingView script are free. There is no checkout on this site.

Not a forecast

Official data arrive with lags and are revised. Past index readings, and their historical alignment with NBER dates, do not predict future economic or market outcomes.

THE INDEX

The US Economic Health Index

The flagship monthly reading. A value of 50 means the latest changes are in line with each series’ trailing ten-year history. It is not a claim that conditions are “normal,” and it is not a forecast. Expansion is 60 or above, slowdown is 40–59, and contraction is below 40.

As of October 2026, the index stood at 40.9, in the slowdown band, using five of six series by design. October 2026 is a month in 2026Q4, so real GDP is not in that month’s composite; the latest official GDP print is the 2026Q3 advance estimate dated October 29, 2026, and a later revision of that quarter is context only. The 12-month all-items CPI-U change is unavailable because BLS did not publish an October 2025 all-items index. A November 2026 sample report is in preparation. Methodology

BEA

Real GDP

Quarterly real gross domestic product, assigned only to the three months of a published quarter. Advance and later estimates are labeled by vintage.

BLS

Unemployment rate

The official civilian unemployment rate, compared with its trailing history rather than treated as a raw level.

BLS

Nonfarm payrolls

Total nonfarm payroll employment, including when the latest print is still a preliminary estimate.

BLS

Consumer price index

CPI-U, converted into a simple change and signed so that faster inflation weighs as a drag on the reading.

Federal Reserve

Effective federal funds rate

The effective federal funds rate, included as a change versus its own recent history.

Federal Reserve

Industrial production

The Federal Reserve Board’s industrial production index. The official unit (for example 2017=100 or 2022=100) is read from the G.17 file, not hard-coded.

CHARTS

A free TradingView indicator, coming soon

We are drafting a free public TradingView script, provisionally called Macro Regime, that would shade official yield-curve and jobless-claims conditions on a chart. It is not published yet, and it will not appear here as a live product until it is actually available.

What it is meant to do

The draft script reads economic series that TradingView already hosts. It is intended as impersonal context — a description of official data on a chart — not a buy or sell signal and not investment advice. When a reviewed version is published, it will be free to use, and we will add the TradingView link on this page.

What it is not

It is not available today. It is not a record of past trading performance, and we do not publish accuracy rates. Official data are revised, and a shaded regime is not a recommendation to take any particular action.
